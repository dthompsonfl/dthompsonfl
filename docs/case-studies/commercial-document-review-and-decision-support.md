# Commercial Document Review & Leadership Decision Support

**Scope:** Independent business projects and planning  
**Focus:** Understanding obligations, presenting options and documenting decisions

## Reading agreements as business decisions

I have worked with commercial documentation involving **master service agreements (MSAs), statements of work (SOWs), nondisclosure agreements (NDAs) and data-processing agreements (DPAs)**.

My work has included comparing proposed terms, identifying questions or changes for review, suggesting redlines, and preparing materials for appropriate counsel or stakeholder review. The practical purpose is to understand scope, handoffs, responsibilities, changes and risk before the work proceeds.

This is **commercial and operations-oriented document experience**, not legal representation. I am not a licensed attorney, and I do not claim that drafts were necessarily negotiated, signed or approved by counsel.

## Turning research into a leadership decision

In 2026, I prepared and presented a customer-growth and measurement proposal to organization leadership. It set out a possible approach and the work involved. After considering the costs and effort, leadership **did not adopt** the proposed implementation.

The result was a decision, **not** a deployed marketing system or a measured revenue increase. I consider it a useful example because good analysis should help people say *no* to a plan when the tradeoffs do not justify it.

## What this work demonstrates

Commercial document review and decision presentations require a similar discipline to purchasing and systems work:

1. Understand the actual obligation or operating objective.
2. Identify ambiguity, dependencies and the people who have authority to decide.
3. Compare practical options, including their effort and risk.
4. Document what was proposed, what was decided and what remains open.

The underlying business documents and presentations are not published because they contain nonpublic material. This case study is limited to my work and the known decision outcome; it does not claim executed contracts, legal qualifications, formal board approval or operational gains.
