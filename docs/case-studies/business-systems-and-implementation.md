# Business Systems & Implementation

**Context:** Independent, project-based development beginning in 2021  
**Focus:** Understanding business requirements and translating them into practical workflows and software

I work from both sides of a business-system problem: the people who have to use the process and the data, permissions and integrations needed to support it. Software is useful when it makes the operation clearer and more reliable, not simply because a new tool can be built.

## 1. Commerce and ERP integration for my own business

For my instrument-repair business, I designed and implemented synchronization work connecting **Shopify Admin GraphQL and ERPNext**. I report using that integration in the business.

The relevant challenge was translating commerce information into operational records so product and business workflows could be managed across systems. This was **my own business integration**, not evidence of a separate enterprise consulting engagement or independently certified uptime.

A minimal historical [Shopify-to-ERP repository](https://github.com/dthompsonfl/shopifysync1) documents the direction of my early work, but it does **not** publicly establish the detailed implementation described here.

## 2. Custom CRM development and an internal pilot

I originated and worked on custom customer-relationship-management systems tailored to business workflows. In one internal feasibility pilot, I explored how a CRM could support client-service and delivery processes.

The pilot was used to examine an approach, not to demonstrate a launched customer product. I do not attach invented user counts, customer contracts, deployments or revenue to it.

## 3. Public examples of workflow-oriented software

- **[Repair Portal](https://github.com/dthompsonfl/repair_portal)** models instrument-service records and steps in Frappe/ERPNext. Its public source includes hooks, permissions and scheduled work. The repository is a view into operational software design, not proof that every documented feature is production-certified.
- **[ECCB application](https://github.com/dthompsonfl/eccb.app)** contains public, member and administrative workflows with role-aware access and background processing. My contribution included requirements and direction; repository content alone does not establish that I personally wrote every feature or certify a particular deployment.
- **[Restaurant POS prototype](https://github.com/dthompsonfl/pos)** explores ordering, payment boundaries, shifts, kitchen workflows and recovery paths. It remains a prototype under production hardening, not a certified live point-of-sale deployment.

## How I approach an implementation

I start by learning who performs the work, what decisions they make and what records must remain trustworthy. I identify exceptions and handoffs before deciding whether a procedure, spreadsheet, integration or application is the appropriate response. Where I use AI-assisted development, I direct, review and test the work rather than implying every line was typed manually.

This is **business-process and implementation experience with project-specific verification limits**, not a blanket claim of enterprise deployments or uninterrupted salaried software-engineering tenure.
