# Career & Engineering Timeline

## Before software development

My career began in business operations rather than inside a traditional software organization.

From 2013 onward, I operated customer-facing businesses where reliability, cash controls, employee workflows, scheduling, customer service, and operational decision-making had immediate financial consequences.

That operating experience became the foundation for how I later approached software: systems need to work for real people under real pressure, not only in idealized workflows.

## 2021 — Shopify and commerce development

I began software development in **2021** through Shopify.

My early work included:

- Shopify theme customization
- storefront behavior
- app and extension work
- product and catalog workflows
- commerce integrations
- ERP synchronization
- merchant-specific business logic
- adapting third-party applications to actual operational requirements

This work taught me to develop inside live commerce environments where software changes could immediately affect customers, transactions, inventory, and revenue.

## 2022–2024 — Integrations and business applications

I progressively moved beyond storefront customization into broader application and integration work.

Areas of growth included:

- APIs and service integrations
- ERP and operational workflows
- Python/Frappe applications
- backend domain modeling
- workflow automation
- data synchronization
- authenticated applications
- administrative tools
- AI/LLM experimentation and integrations

The [Repair Portal](https://github.com/dthompsonfl/repair_portal) is representative of this stage: a domain-heavy operations application modeling intake, inspection, planning, repair execution, parts/materials, QA, and delivery.

## 2025 — Full-stack and platform architecture

My work expanded into larger systems with multiple user surfaces and clearer platform boundaries.

I increasingly focused on:

- Next.js and React applications
- TypeScript across frontend and backend
- relational database design
- Prisma
- authentication and RBAC
- administrative control planes
- multi-role workflows
- queues and background jobs
- payment integrations
- mobile architecture
- testing and release controls

The [Emerald Coast Community Band Platform](https://github.com/dthompsonfl/eccb.app) demonstrates public, authenticated, and administrative application surfaces with Next.js, React, TypeScript, Prisma, Better Auth, Redis/BullMQ, CMS workflows, and fine-grained access control.

## 2025–2026 — Restaurant systems, SaaS, mobile, and reliability

My current work is centered on larger operational platforms, including restaurant technology and SaaS products.

Key areas include:

- POS architecture
- kitchen display workflows
- online ordering
- menu and pricing configuration
- payment flows
- employee and role permissions
- shifts and tender reconciliation
- operational devices
- offline-aware mobile systems
- server-authoritative state
- workflow orchestration
- idempotency and retries
- migration safety
- release gates
- AI-enabled workflows
- administrative control planes

The public [Enterprise POS Android](https://github.com/dthompsonfl/pos) repository documents many of these concerns explicitly, including payment-provider abstractions, offline persistence, Room migrations, release safeguards, sync architecture, hardware boundaries, backend scaffolding, and fail-closed production behavior.

## Current direction

I am most interested in software where backend correctness directly affects real operations:

- restaurant technology
- workflow automation
- high-consequence business processes
- payments
- distributed operational state
- platform engineering
- autonomous/agentic systems with deterministic guardrails
- software for small and local businesses

My software-development timeline begins in 2021. I do not present my earlier business experience as software-engineering experience; instead, I use it as operational context that strengthens the way I design software today.
